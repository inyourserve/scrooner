Design guideline · v3

An editorial ledger, not a SaaS template.

Serif for editorial authority. Monospace for every number, because a research tool should look like it computes rather than decorates. A warm paper canvas instead of cool SaaS gray-mint. Shadows and gradients used almost nowhere — restraint is the premium signal, not decoration.

01

Posture & anti-patterns

v1 was flat and under-considered. v2 over-corrected by throwing soft shadows and a gradient blob at everything — which is its own cliché: it's the default look of AI-generated SaaS templates (Framer/v0/Lovable output), instantly recognizable and not premium. v3 starts from a different question: what does a 20-year designer reach for when shadows-and-gradients aren't the answer? Typography, material honesty, and restraint.

Rejected — generic AI-SaaS tells
  • Headline-left / floating-gradient-blob-card-right hero
  • Colorful pill buttons for every example or tag
  • Soft shadow on every card, table, and button
  • Gradient fills on primary buttons
  • Mint-green-on-cool-gray palette
  • Bootstrap-style bordered feature-card grid for "how it works"
  • Uniform 12–20px rounded corners on everything
Scrooner's actual identity
  • Full-width editorial masthead, figure/caption convention for product proof
  • Text links and hairline-divided lists over pill soup
  • Flat surfaces with hairline borders; shadow reserved for truly floating UI
  • Solid ink-green buttons, no gradient
  • Warm paper canvas, near-black ink, deep forest accent
  • Numbered editorial sequence (01 / 02 / 03), not a card grid
  • Sharper, mixed radii — ledger tables near-square, panels lightly rounded

02

Warm paper instead of cool SaaS gray. Deep forest green instead of SaaS mint — it reads as a ledger stamp, not a startup logo. Brand color means action or selection, never "this company is good."

03

Typography — the actual signature

Three typefaces, each with one job. Fraunces (serif) carries editorial weight — headlines, section titles, company names. Inter (sans) carries the interface — body copy, labels, controls. IBM Plex Mono carries every number, ticker, date, and formula reference — a research tool should look like it computes, not like it's narrating.

04

Elevation & radius — used sparingly

Shadow is reserved for surfaces that are genuinely floating above the page: a dropdown, a popover, a modal, or the sticky header after scroll. A resting card, a table, a button — none of these float, so none of them get a shadow. This restraint is what separates a considered interface from a decorated one.

Radius

6px — buttons, inputs, badges 8px — panels, metric cells 14px — dialogs only 0–1px — ledger tables (near-square)

05

Spacing & grid

Reading column

Prose and hero copy sit in a ~640px column. Tables and figures break out full-width. This contraction/expansion rhythm is a classic editorial layout device — it is what makes a page feel composed rather than stacked.

13

Voice & copy

Direct, calm, specific, transparent about limits. Never promotional at the expense of truth.

Use
  • "Calculated from company filings using formula version 1."
  • "Excluded because ROIC was unavailable for the selected period."
  • "Rule-based checklist; not an investment recommendation."
  • "Passed 4 of 4 selected criteria."
Avoid
  • "AI found the best stocks."
  • "Real time" for delayed data.
  • "No data" when a precise reason is known.
  • "Safe," "guaranteed," "undervalued" without a disclosed rule.

14

Accessibility — WCAG 2.2 AA

  • Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 (3:1 for large text) — verify serif/mono at small sizes too
  • Visible focus on every interactive element
  • Full keyboard operation, including filters and drawers
  • No information conveyed by color alone
  • Target size ≥ 44×44px where practicable
  • Reflow at 320px without 2D scrolling (tables excepted)
  • Negative values announced as negative, not just colored
  • Sort state exposed via aria-sort

15

Design review checklist

  • No shadow on a surface that isn't actually floating
  • No gradient anywhere
  • Every number is set in mono with tabular figures
  • Every value has a unit, a period, and a source
  • Accent color stays at or below the 10% ceiling
  • Reading column contracts for prose, expands for tables
  • No unsupported claim of AI understanding or complete coverage
  • Works by keyboard, at 320px, and at 200% zoom

16

Sources & rationale

Grounded in a full page-by-page read of The UI/UX Playbook (144 pp.) and How to Design Better UI Components 3.0 (197 pp.), plus doc/design/10_Scrooner_Product_Design_Framework.md and screener.in's structural patterns. v2 applied those books' elevation chapters too literally — shadow and gradient as the primary lever — and read as generic. v3 keeps the underlying craft (material honesty, real hierarchy, considered depth) but expresses it through typography, color, and editorial layout instead, which is what those same books' hierarchy and consistency chapters actually argue for once shadow/gradient stop being the default answer.
